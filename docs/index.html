Motivation

Why Start from Gaussian Noise?

Actions and scenes change continuously, and the robot already knows its recent past. Δ-flow starts the flow there, so the network only learns a small change Δ.

Gaussian source

Noise → future

  • Starts far from the target
  • ~10 steps; 1 step is jerky
Δ-flow

History + noise → future

  • Starts next to the target
  • 1 step, smooth across chunks

Ingredient 1

Up to 6× Faster Training

On LIBERO, ImageWAM reaches 90% success in one epoch instead of six, and Fast-WAM reaches 95% in two epochs instead of eight.

Ingredient 2

Stronger Generalization

Tested on LIBERO-Plus (7 perturbation types) without extra fine-tuning. ImageWAM rises from 82.49% to 88.55%, state of the art without embodied-data pretraining; the biggest gains are robot initialization (52.90 → 73.94) and object layout (81.31 → 88.39). π0.5 rises from 80.70% to 84.53%.

Ingredient 3

One-Step, Smooth Inference

Complete policy call on an A100. ImageWAM 296 → 119 ms (2.49×), Fast-WAM 858 → 193 ms (4.45×), π0.5 83 → 54 ms (1.54×).

One-step Gaussian sampling makes actions jerky (4.6×). Δ-flow stays smooth: each chunk starts from where the robot is.

Design Lessons

What else matters when putting Δ-flow into a foundation model.

01 · Fast-WAM

Predict fewer future frames

Over the same 32-step horizon, predicting 4 future frames beats predicting 8.

+5.19pp LIBERO-Plus
02 · ImageWAM

Stop action gradients into the world model

Blocking action-loss gradients sharpens future-frame prediction (PSNR 15.04 → 20.86 dB) and helps under shift. Share features, not gradients.

+1.7pp LIBERO-Plus
03 · ImageWAM

Endpoint loss alone almost suffices

Endpoint consistency alone nearly matches FM + EC; flow matching still speeds up early training (78.10 → 89.90 after one epoch).

87.30vs 87.37 LIBERO-Plus
04 · ImageWAM

Use local delta actions

Local delta actions beat 7D absolute actions most clearly under distribution shift (85.26 vs 61.07).
